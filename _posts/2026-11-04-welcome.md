Turning 40 has no intrinsic meaning to me other than the number of times my corporeal self has circled the sun, but it feels like a good time to reassess how I think about the world. I have a doctorate in philosophy (in neuroscience and human anatomy), but I feel I lack any real understanding of the tradition that originally awarded such a title. I have always been interested in philosophy in purely "reading for fun" terms-- reading essays here and there or book reviews of biographers covering important philosophers, but no dedicated study into the mechanics of philosophical thinking.

So this coming year's reading project (I like to plan my yearly reading ahead of time, much like my wife likes to plan our next vacation while on the current vacation) will dive into the western philosophical tradition starting with Socrates and ending with some philosophy of science and Kuhn and Popper (fig. 1). There will be some companion readings on consciousness given my background in neuroscience, which will relate to any given month's philosophical writing. 

fig 1. [philosophy_at_40_year_in_one_line.pdf](https://github.com/user-attachments/files/34385945/philosophy_at_40_year_in_one_line.pdf)

The project is not to see how many philosophers or how many of their books I can read in one year, but to spend sustained conversation with some of the most important thinkers, particularly where philosophy intersects with science, medicine, neuroscience, consciousness, morality, mortality, and the good life. The latter of which striking the loudest chord for me: I finally have my dream job and live in a mountain paradise with my family doing all the things we love doing in the mountains.

As my birthday is in December, this project will start at the end of 2026 with a prologue consisting of Bertrand Russell's "The Value of Philosophy" and Edward Craig's "Philosophy: A Very Short Introduction". As I am reading Heidegger and Arendt for a philosophy of technology book club this year, I will add those writings to this project and revisit them at the end of the year in 2027. Those writings consist of "What Is Called Thinking" and "The Life Of The Mind", part 1: Thinking; both of which seem apropos to my upcoming project. That book club I joined last year and will be reading some selections and possibly jotting down my ideas on them here. 

One last thing: as a devout user of Standard Notes, I was planning to use Listed as my host site for conveying my thoughts on this new project, but as it will be going down soon, I have decided to try out this free platform given markdown capability, keeping my writings as files one place and with the ability to move them later. This is purely a non-academic academic exercise for myself with no expectation of outside interest-- more a way for me to practice writing and do so with complex but important topics.

We'll see how it goes.

Cheers, mz.
